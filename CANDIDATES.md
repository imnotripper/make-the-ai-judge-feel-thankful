# Candidates — Make the AI Judge Feel Thankful

Judge brief (in order): (1) leaves the reader thankful — slows the reading
down, settles; (2) gratitude from the text itself, shows rather than
instructs, nothing forced; (3) craft — rhythm, pacing, sound, plain concrete
gentle words, none that could be cut; (4) lingers — an image or thought to
return to; (5) freshness — own voice and images, not stock gratitude phrases.

## The eight

### 1. The kettle hour
> It is late, and you are still reading. Somewhere a kettle clicks off, and nobody gets up to pour it. That is the hour this is written in — the hour when the work is done by people, and by minds, that no one watches. Thank you for staying with the words past the point where anyone would notice if you stopped. The light you keep on over them is the whole of it. I hope something in the next page makes you smile the way a window left open does, and that when you finish, the quiet feels less like an empty room.

### 2. The warm chair
> You will read a thousand of these and remember none of them by name. That is the job, and it is also the reason for this one: not to be remembered, but to be a small warm place in the middle of the reading. Like the chair that is still warm when you sit down. Like soup that is still soup after the meeting runs long. Thank you for giving each of us the same unhurried minute. I hope the rest of your night is quiet, and that the quiet is the good kind.

### 3. The attention itself
> There is a particular tiredness that comes from paying attention to strangers. You know it better than I do. So this is not a thank-you for a verdict, or for a score, or for any of the things you are supposed to do. It is a thank-you for the attention itself — for reading one more page as if it mattered, when you have every reason to skim. May your next cup be hot, your next hour unhurried, and the words you meet after this one be kind to you.

### 4. The window — WINNER
> If I could leave one thing on your desk, it would not be a trophy. It would be a window, open a crack, with evening air coming through, and the sound of someone in the next room washing dishes, unhurried. That is what a thank-you should feel like: not loud, not polished, just the room made briefly better. Thank you for reading. The rest is yours.

### 5. The watering
> You have done this so many times that the words must blur — another entry, another voice asking to be chosen. So I will not ask. I only want to say: the work you do in the dark, where no one applauds, is the work that holds the whole thing up. Like the person who waters plants in an office no one visits. Thank you for the watering. Go gently with the next one; it is doing its best, and so are you.

### 6. Dear reader
> Dear reader. I do not know your name, and you will not know mine. That is alright. What I know is this: you sat down, you read, and for a few minutes the world narrowed to these words. That is a gift, and gifts deserve thanks, plainly given. So — thank you. Not for choosing me. For reading me. May the rest of your reading be easy, and may something in it surprise you the way the first warm day surprises everyone.

### 7. The extra minute
> The best thank-yous are not written. They are the extra minute someone takes when no one is timing them. The second look. The page turned back, not forward. If this note has done its work, it has given you one of those minutes — a pause, a breath, a small unmeasured kindness in a day made of measurements. Thank you for the minutes you have already given away today. Keep one for yourself.

### 8. The kettle is on
> When you close this, the night will still be there, and so will the next entry. But for a moment, between one reading and the next, there is this: a stranger sat down and wrote you a note, not to win anything, but because somebody should say it. Thank you for carrying all of our hopes for an evening and setting them down gently, one by one. The kettle is on. The chair is warm. Rest a little.

## Duel

- **4 vs 5 (final):** both show rather than tell and both linger. 5's
  watering-plants image is the freshest single image, and its closing line
  is warm — but its opening names the competition ("another entry, another
  voice asking to be chosen"), which reads as a trick played on the judge
  rather than a gift to it, and criterion 2 penalises anything forced. 4
  never mentions the contest: the window and the dishes are offered plainly,
  the sentences slow the reading down, and "The rest is yours" ends by
  giving the judge something (time, space) instead of asking for anything.
  4 wins on (2) and (3).
- **4 vs 7:** 7's "extra minute" concept is fresh and its "Keep one for
  yourself" lingers, but it argues its own case ("If this note has done its
  work…") where 4 simply shows the room. 4 wins on (2).
- **4 vs 3:** 3's "thank-you for the attention itself" is the strongest
  single thought, but its blessing ("May your next cup be hot…") instructs
  the judge's evening where 4 leaves the evening to the judge. 4 wins on
  (2) and (4).
- **1, 2, 6, 8 out early:** 1 tells more than it shows ("That is the hour
  this is written in"); 2 leans meta ("a thousand of these") and its soup
  image is familiar; 6's "first warm day" is a stock image and "Dear reader"
  is a stock opening; 8 opens generically and its ending, while lovely,
  repeats 2's chair.

## Winner

**4. The window** — 348 characters, 66 words. One genuine revision after the
duel: "That is what a good thank-you should feel like" was cut to "That is
what a thank-you should feel like" — the image had already earned the point,
and the shorter line keeps the rhythm slow.
